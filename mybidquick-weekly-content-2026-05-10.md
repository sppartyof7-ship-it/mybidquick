**Reddit Post 2 — r/smallbusiness**

**Title:** Lesson from running a service business: charge for value delivered, not seats opened

I run an exterior cleaning company and recently built a SaaS tool on the side. Going through the pricing exercise reminded me of something I think a lot of service-business folks miss when picking software.

Most B2B software charges by "seats" or by month — meaning you pay the same in your slowest week as in your busiest. That model is great for the software company and terrible for a seasonal business.

When I priced my own tool, I made it pay-per-lead instead of monthly. $2 when a real customer submits a quote, $0 when they don't. It aligns my revenue with the customer's revenue, which is how it should feel to use a tool you actually depend on.

The point isn't a pitch — it's that next time you're shopping for software, ask the vendor: "Does the way you charge me line up with the value I'm getting?" If the answer is "no, I pay the same whether I use it or not," that's a red flag worth pushing on.
