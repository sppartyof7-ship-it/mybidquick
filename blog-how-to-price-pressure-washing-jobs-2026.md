# How to Price Pressure Washing Jobs in 2026 (From an Owner With 18 Years in the Game)

**Author:** Tim Sullivan, Owner of TKS Exterior Cleaning, Managing Partner at Cloute Cleaning (since 2008) & Founder of MyBidQuick
**Target Keyword:** how to price pressure washing jobs
**Secondary Keywords:** pressure washing pricing guide, pressure washing rates 2026, how much to charge for pressure washing
**Meta Description:** An exterior cleaning company owner with 18 years of experience breaks down how he prices pressure washing jobs — from the hourly billing mistakes he made in 2008 to the package pricing model that gets him $1,200 tickets today.

---

When I started in exterior cleaning back in 2008, I had no clue how to price a job. And most of the people I talk to in this industry still don't.

I've spent 18 years figuring this out the hard way — underpricing myself, confusing my customers, and leaving money on the table. Here's everything I've learned about pricing pressure washing jobs so you don't have to make the same mistakes I did.

## We Started Billing by the Hour. It Was a Mess.

When I first started, we bid most of our jobs by the hour. We'd figure out an hourly rate, guess how long a job should take, and multiply. Simple, right?

It was a disaster. There were a lot of *feelings* involved in the pricing — and it fluctuated constantly.

Here's where it really fell apart: I had one employee who was fast. He'd knock out a job in two hours. Then I'd send a different two-person crew to a similar house and they'd take four hours. The client who got me? They paid for two hours of work. The client who got the other crew? Four hours. Same type of house, wildly different prices.

It got worse. We had customers who started requesting only me because they knew they'd get charged less. When we sent anyone else, the bill doubled and they weren't happy about it.

We were underpricing ourselves and making it confusing for our clients at the same time. That's a bad combination.

## How We Price Jobs Now (And Why Packages Changed Everything)

These days we use a combination of flat rate pricing, per-square-foot rates, per-window pricing — and then we lean *heavily* into packages.

Packages make the world go round. Here's why.

If you're doing a house washing, you might as well do the windows while you're there. If you're doing windows, might as well knock out the gutters too. Our whole goal is to hit $1,200 tickets, and the easiest way to get there isn't to just jack up your prices. It's to add value.

We can add value *and* cut costs at the same time because we're already on the property. No extra drive time. No extra gas. No leaving one job to go set up at another. If we can be a one-stop shop for a customer's exterior cleaning needs, it works out perfect for them and it works out perfect for us.

That's the key most people miss: you don't get to $1,200 by charging $1,200 for a house wash. You get there by bundling a house wash, window cleaning, and gutter cleaning into a package that makes the customer feel like they're getting a deal — because they are.

## The Biggest Pricing Mistake I See Other Owners Make

Not being confident in their pricing. Being all over the place.

Here's what happens: you quote one neighbor $560 for a house wash. You quote the neighbor two doors down $660 for basically the same house. They talk. They always talk. And now you look unprofessional and one of them feels ripped off.

Consistent pricing fixes this. When your pricing is dialed in, *anyone* on your team can quote a job and you can be 100% sure the number is right. You're not relying on gut feelings or whoever happened to answer the phone.

The other big thing — if raising your prices is a chore, you won't do it. If you have to go through every service line and manually adjust things, you'll put it off for months. You need to be able to have ticklers in place that can raise or lower your prices across the board in the blink of an eye. That way when costs go up (and they always go up), you actually adjust instead of eating the difference.

## "The Other Guy Quoted Me $150"

I literally had this conversation today. It never goes away.

Here's how I handle it: I don't trash the other company. I just tell it like it is.

Everybody's going to say they're the best. We truly are, and I mean that — we've been in this industry for 18 years. We study. We train. We invest in our employees. We take your job seriously and we know exactly what to do to make you happy.

But I'm honest with people. About 50% of the guys quoting $150 do great work. The other 50% don't. So the question becomes: would you rather spend $150 on a company you're not quite confident can do the job? Or spend $350 with a company where you don't even have to worry about it?

The way I put it: the only reason you're even going to know we were there is because your house is cleaner when we're done.

That line works because it's true. We show up, we do the work right, and we leave. No drama, no callbacks, no "hey can you come back and redo the south side." That peace of mind is worth the price difference, and most homeowners get it once you frame it that way.

## How Tiered Packages (Standard / Premium / Platinum) Changed Our Average Ticket

Before we had tiers, I used to just try to sell everyone the premium service. The problem? Sticker shock. People would see the price, get scared, and not know what to do. A lot of them just wouldn't book at all.

Adding a Standard tier changed the game. Standard is our baseline — that's the level of service we live by. We're always going to do a great job at that level. But Premium and Platinum? That's for people who expect even more detail.

Here's a real example using gutter cleaning:

**Standard gutter cleaning:** We scoop everything out, take all the debris off, and we check your downspouts to make sure they're clear. You're going to have a clean gutter. There might still be some leaves and sludge stuck in spots because we're not flushing them out, but the job is done well.

**Platinum gutter cleaning:** We go through every square inch of your gutters and flush them out completely. You could almost eat out of your gutters when we're done. We power-flush through every downspout to make sure they don't even leak. If they do leak, we're giving you a detailed report. We're checking your roof — letting you know if it needs to be cleaned, if you've got shingle issues, all of it. That platinum service is a full inspection on top of the cleaning.

We only send certain crews for Premium and Platinum jobs. Our Standard guys are great — they crush route work and they're consistent. But the Premium and Platinum crews are doing the above-and-beyond stuff that some customers really want.

And here's the thing: some people don't want to pay for that. That's totally okay. They just want their place cleaned and they want it done right. Standard handles that. But giving people the *option* to upgrade means the people who DO want the best will pay for it — and your average ticket goes up without you having to hard-sell anyone.

## One Thing I Wish Someone Told Me About Pricing When I Started

Your time means something. Your effort means something. Charge accordingly.

Don't short yourself. I spent years undercharging because I was afraid of losing jobs. You know what happened? I won a bunch of jobs I didn't make any money on. I was busy but I wasn't profitable, and that's a terrible place to be.

The cleaning companies that survive long-term aren't the cheapest ones. They're the ones who price for profit and deliver enough value to justify it.
